HIPAA & BAA Info

Effective November 2, 2026

If we touch patient information for your practice, there's a signed agreement first and access limited to what the work needs. Here's how that works.

Who is who

If you run a healthcare practice or treatment program, you are the Covered Entity under HIPAA. When we handle protected health information (PHI) for you, we are a Business Associate. Your practice stays responsible for its own HIPAA compliance. We support it, and we're bound by contract and by the law to handle PHI properly.

The BAA comes first

A Business Associate Agreement (BAA) is signed before anyone on our team gets access to PHI, patient inboxes, scheduling, or EHR systems. That is true for every healthcare engagement. The BAA spells out what we may do with PHI, what safeguards we use, how we report problems, and what happens to PHI when the work ends.

To start the BAA process, email shineyourlightonit@gmail.com.

Until a BAA is signed, please don't send us PHI, not through the website, the Personality Match, a contact form, a scheduling note, or email. If you send it by mistake, tell us right away and we'll help you work out next steps.

How we handle PHI

  • Minimum necessary. Each person gets access only to what their tasks require, and no more.
  • Role-based access. Tools and logins are set up by role, and we use your systems under accounts you control where we can.
  • Protected credentials. Passwords and access details are exchanged and stored in encrypted, access-controlled ways. Not in plain email.
  • US-based team. Every person who works on your account is US-based. We don't offshore healthcare work, and PHI doesn't leave the country.
  • HIPAA-supporting tools. We pick tools that offer a BAA and the right security tier for the job.
  • Confidentiality. Everyone on our team signs confidentiality terms.
  • Incident plan. We have a process for suspected incidents, and the BAA sets the timing for notifying you. In every case, that is without unreasonable delay and no later than HIPAA requires.
  • Clean offboarding. When an engagement or a team member's role ends, we remove access and audit that it's gone.

Substance use disorder programs and 42 CFR Part 2

Programs that provide substance use disorder treatment may also be covered by 42 CFR Part 2, which adds protections for those records on top of HIPAA. If that's you, we'll address it in the agreement, commonly through a Qualified Service Organization Agreement (QSOA), alongside the BAA.

What "HIPAA-aware" means

We use "HIPAA-aware" to describe how we work: our practices are built to support a Covered Entity's compliance. It is not a certification. No government body certifies a vendor as "HIPAA compliant," and we don't claim one did. We also aren't a law firm. Nothing here is legal advice, and your own counsel or compliance officer should make the calls about your obligations.

If you're a patient

We don't keep records for our own use. We only handle PHI on behalf of the practice that engaged us. To see, correct, or ask about your health records, contact that practice directly. If you think your information was mishandled, you can also file a complaint with the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services Office for Civil Rights.
